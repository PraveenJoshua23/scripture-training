**Subject:** Licence enquiry — NASB1995 full text for a non-commercial Scripture memorisation application

---

Dear Permissions Team,

I am writing to enquire about licensing the full text of the New American
Standard Bible, 1995 edition, for use in a non-commercial Scripture
memorisation application.

**What I am requesting**

A licence to reproduce the complete NASB1995 text — all 66 books, approximately
31,000 verses — within a web application used for Scripture memorisation. I am
requesting the 1995 edition specifically, rather than the 2020 edition, because
that is the text our group has been memorising.

I recognise this goes well beyond your general quotation allowance and would
constitute a full-text licence rather than a permission to quote. I would be
grateful to know what form of agreement, terms, and fees would apply, and I am
prepared to enter into a formal licence agreement.

**How the text would be used**

The application helps members of [CHURCH / GROUP NAME] memorise Scripture. It
presents one verse at a time and offers four practice modes: typing the verse
out, filling in missing words, reciting it aloud, and listening to it read by
the browser's speech synthesis. It also provides a self-test over a chosen
range of verses and tracks which verses a user has completed.

The full text of a verse is displayed during practice, since the user is
learning to reproduce it accurately. The application is a study aid; it is not
a Bible reader, and it does not present the text for continuous reading.

**How the text would be stored**

I want to be direct about this, as I understand it is material to your policy.
The verse text would be stored as data files bundled with the application and
read by the user's browser — an electronic retrieval system as your terms
describe it. The text would not be exposed through a public API, offered as a
download, or made available for reuse by other applications. I am willing to
implement whatever technical safeguards against bulk extraction you consider
appropriate.

**Scope and distribution**

- Non-commercial. The application is not sold, carries no advertising, and has no paid tier.
- Access is by private link, shared within [CHURCH / GROUP NAME]. It is not listed publicly or indexed by search engines.
- Expected users: approximately [NUMBER] people.
- The Scripture text would not be redistributed, sub-licensed, or provided to third parties in any form.

If a licence covering the complete Bible is not something you grant for a use
of this size, I would welcome the option of a narrower grant — for example a
single book or a defined set of books — and would be glad to discuss what scope
you would be comfortable with.

**Attribution**

I will display the full copyright notice you require for digital media,
including the clickable link to www.Lockman.org, in the footer of every page of
the application. I am happy to place it anywhere else you specify, and to
submit the application for your review before it is used.

**Current status**

The application is built and running on a public-domain translation while this
enquiry is pending. No NASB text has been copied or used at any point. If a
licence is granted, the NASB1995 text would replace the public-domain text; if
it is not, we will continue with the public-domain text.

Please let me know what further detail you need. I am happy to provide a
demonstration of the application or answer any questions about how the text
would be handled.

## Notes before sending

- **The postal address is required.** Lockman will not grant permission without a mailing address, so do not omit it.
- **Say 1995 explicitly**, as above. Lockman actively promotes the 2020 edition and may otherwise assume that is what you want.
- **Expect a licence agreement, not a yes/no reply.** A full-Bible request is a different category from a permission to quote — it puts you alongside their commercial digital partners. The letter is framed as an enquiry that invites terms, rather than a request expecting a simple grant.
- **The fallback paragraph is deliberate.** Asking for the whole Bible and offering to accept a narrower grant gives them an easy way to say "not that, but this" instead of simply declining.
- **Do not soften the storage paragraph.** Electronic storage is the part their policy scrutinises most, and it matters more at full-Bible scale than for one book.
- **Adjust the user count honestly.** If this may grow beyond a single congregation, say so now rather than seeking an expansion later.
- If the answer is no, or slow, the Berean Standard Bible is a modern formal-equivalence translation that is free for any use and needs no permission.
